# Typography

For website design images as well as other outputs, choose fonts for the purpose, reader, brand and language. Novelty alone is not a criterion; existing specified or standard fonts may be suitable. Define heading, body and control-label roles. Establish hierarchy through size, weight, leading and spacing before adding font families. Judge multiple fonts by compatible apparent size, weight and tone, not contrast alone.

Check actual mixed Japanese/Latin copy for glyph appearance, baselines, punctuation and wrapping. Do not uniformly apply Latin tracking or line-length conventions to Japanese. Avoid unjustified extremely light/heavy, condensed or italic text and tight tracking. Check heading/body readability at intended display sizes and record accepted fonts, weights and layout values in templates.

Image-generation prompts should specify text roles and font characteristics. Generated images do not guarantee a named font, exact copy or correct glyphs; distinguish visual suggestions from finalized typography. For exact text, separate image assets and typeset text through templates or HTML/CSS. Check Japanese and Latin for mismatched tone, glyphs, weight, spacing and hierarchy; fix supported problems before visual acceptance.
