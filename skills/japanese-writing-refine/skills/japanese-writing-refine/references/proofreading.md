# Proofreading and ordinary refinement

1. **Accuracy:** Find typos, particle or honorific errors, and inconsistent names/terms for the same concept. Respect intentional dialogue, dialect and informal forms.
2. **Readability:** Before replacing words, trace each predicate's actor/object and the correspondence of modifiers, references, conditions and negation. Prioritize shifting actors, long modifier/noun chains and nested conditions/negation that require rereading. Reorder, punctuate or split only when correspondence remains intact. Length or comma count alone is insufficient; do not fill natural omitted subjects or choose the nearest noun when multiple referents exist.
3. **Information density:** Reduce repetition and empty modifiers that add no meaning. Retain useful repetition and established loanwords. Restate literal translations only where they read unnaturally in Japanese.
4. **Sentence stance:** Check plain/polite register separately from whether a clause explains, requests, establishes a rule or describes a plan. Do not turn advice into the writer's commitment, a required step into an optional recommendation or actual behavior into capability. Ending variety must not change function; retain appropriate mixed register and honorifics.
5. **Evidence alignment:** Flag only interpretations that exceed facts in the supplied text. Do not pretend to conduct external research or expand into an independent argument audit.

For ordinary refinement of practical prose, read headings and paragraph openings together for topic transitions, prerequisite ordering and duplicate explanation. Explain only terms the reader needs, using roles established in the source. Do not universally convert headings to conclusions, lists to prose or paragraphs to a uniform shape. Do not add this structural pass to proofreading-only or limited completed-novel review.

For AI-like practical prose, inspect empty evaluation, unclear evidence attribution, mismatched logical connections, unnatural formulaic lists, monotonous endings and canned openings/closings. Fix actual awkwardness only; preserve appropriate honorifics, technical terms, lists and repetition.

Reconsider metaphors or formulas when the reader cannot tell who does what. Preserve objective tool behavior and established idioms; fix only unsuitable personification or literal-translation phrasing. An unspecified failure must not become a specific integrity error; an unnoticed event must not become a guarantee that no notification was sent. Removing a lead-in such as “重要なのは” or a negative contrast must preserve its meaningful evaluation, negation and contrast.

Separate certain errors from style-dependent suggestions. New examples not supplied by the writer require confirmation rather than invention.
