# Japanese wording cues reported on X and supplied by the user

Load this reference only for the broader wording review routed from [lexical-cues.md](lexical-cues.md). These are prompts to inspect meaning, audience and evidence. They are not banned words, an AI-authorship test, a frequency list or a replacement map. A writer may have chosen any of these expressions independently; [one X writer reports exactly that experience][X-counter]. Check the whole sentence before changing a cue. Preserve quotations, names, code, established project terms, technical precision, factual claims and material promises. If the source cannot support a clearer claim, keep the claim and flag what needs confirmation.

The C entries below come from specific public X posts whose bodies and dates were checked through FxTwitter's status API. The service's documented search endpoint returned 404 during this investigation, so discovery used other public search results. The sample is selective. These posts establish individual impressions, not measured AI usage or a reliable way to identify authorship. The fourteen self-PR examples all come from **one** writer's X article about freelance applications; apply their questions only to comparable claims. The S entries are examples **supplied by the user**. A second X link, where present, never removes that provenance or implies that the X poster originated the example.

For each cue, the “action” is conditional on the original material. Do not change a word merely because it matches the table. When an effect, action or promise remains unclear, keep its substantive wording and flag the missing facts; an equally vague synonym such as 効果を発揮する is not an improvement over 効く. If a passage combines a dispensable flourish or empty lead-in with an unresolved material claim, remove the dispensable part and separately flag the claim; do not invent the missing fact. Where a full phrase includes a general cue already in [lexical-cues.md](lexical-cues.md), make one context decision, not two successive rewrites.

## FxTwitter-verified X reports: C01-C35

### C01-C03: ordinary words used figuratively

[X1] is one poster's sweeping impression, not proof that these ordinary words identify AI writing. [X10] objects to an effect without a specified target.

| ID | Cue | Inspect when | Keep when | Safe action | X source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C01 | 効く | The target, size or mechanism of an asserted effect is unclear. | The target is plain, as in a documented treatment or measured intervention. | Name the supported effect and target; otherwise keep 効く and flag the ambiguity. | [X1], [X10] |
| C02 | 殴る | A figurative impact claim adds heat but no discernible event. | Literal violence or a deliberate, suitable colloquial metaphor is meant. | Remove expendable drama or state the actual supported impact. | [X1] |
| C03 | 壊れる | A dramatic failure claim does not say what failed. | A device, process or specified expectation actually failed. | Name the observed failure if documented; otherwise flag it. | [X1] |

### C04-C05: empathy and care promises

[X2] is one person's concern about repeated vague phrasing in expert health communication. It does not question actual care described concretely.

| ID | Cue | Inspect when | Keep when | Safe action | X source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C04 | そっと寄り添う | A support promise omits the actor, action or recipient, especially in health prose. | A real interaction or clearly described care supports the wording. | State only the documented support; keep and flag an unresolved promise. | [X2] |
| C05 | 優しく整える | An expert claim hides what is changed and what “gentle” means. | The manner and concrete action are known and matter to the reader. | Name the supported action and its limits; flag an unknown effect. | [X2] |

### C06-C15: slogans, contrasts and openings

[X3], [X4], [X5] and [X6] report personal reactions to formulaic prose. A real contrast, priority, referent or personal judgment may need the same form.

| ID | Cue | Inspect when | Keep when | Safe action | X source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C06 | これが本質です | The asserted central distinction was never defined or argued. | The preceding account identifies a real distinguishing feature. | State that feature if known; otherwise retain and flag the claim. | [X3] |
| C07 | 見るべきは | A slogan implies priority without a decision criterion. | A real task has a justified first thing to inspect. | Name the criterion and object when supplied. | [X3] |
| C08 | 数字で殴る | An aggressive metaphor stands in for actual evidence. | A deliberately informal voice and real figures justify it. | Present the supplied numbers and their limits plainly if appropriate. | [X3] |
| C09 | 〇〇を置くと | An abstract “place” hides whether the writer means assume, define or insert. | Physical placement or an established technical use is intended. | Clarify the actual relation only when the source establishes it. | [X3] |
| C10 | 静かに、そして確かに | Mood and certainty imply a change without observation. | Quietness and the claimed trend are both documented or deliberately literary. | Remove a dispensable flourish; do not invent a trend. | [X4] |
| C11 | AではなくBです | The contrast forces a false either-or or overstates certainty. | Evidence really excludes A and supports B. | Explain the supported relation; leave uncertain distinctions qualified. | [X4] |
| C12 | これ、〇〇です | “This” has no clear referent in prose. | A visible item or immediately preceding passage identifies it. | Name the referent if supplied. | [X5] |
| C13 | 私は〜と思います | Repetition of a first-person frame hides the actual view. | Personal judgment and its uncertainty matter. | Remove only a redundant frame; preserve ownership and certainty. | [X5] |
| C14 | 重要なのは | A generic importance frame lacks a reason or comparison. | The priority follows from stated criteria. | Name the supported priority or reason; otherwise flag it. | [X6] |
| C15 | Aではない。Bである | Contrast manufactures a revelation without a true distinction. | A and B are materially distinct in the source. | State the actual distinction and evidence; do not erase a real contrast. | [X6] |

### C18-C21: qualifications and obligation formulas

[X8] describes a subjective objection to a long qualifying frame; [X9] is advice about stiff prose. Required caveats, actual obligations and list introductions remain useful.

| ID | Cue | Inspect when | Keep when | Safe action | X source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C18 | ただし、単純に〇〇という観点だけで比較すると | A long qualifier obscures the actual comparison or caveat. | The restricted comparison is necessary for accuracy. | State the real comparison scope more directly if supplied. | [X8] |
| C19 | 〜することが重要です | “Important” substitutes for a reason or priority. | The source shows why the action matters. | Give the stated reason if known; do not create one. | [X9] |
| C20 | 〜する必要があります | Necessity is asserted without an obligation or condition. | A law, procedure, dependency or stated goal requires it. | Keep the necessary action; if its basis is unknown, flag it. | [X9] |
| C21 | 以下の通りです | A lead-in delays a point and no list follows. | It introduces an actual list or precise enumeration. | Omit only an empty lead-in. | [X9] |

### C22-C35: freelancer self-PR

All fourteen examples in this group come from [one X article][X12]. Its critique concerns generic, insufficiently specific claims or poor fit to a freelance job; it does not establish a general AI frequency or ban for these words.

| ID | Cue | Inspect when | Keep when | Safe action | X source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C22 | 貢献 | A freelancer pitch does not say what work or result is offered. | The contribution is identified by tasks or evidence. | Name the supported contribution; ask for missing details. | [X12] |
| C23 | 最大限の価値 | A pitch promises an undefined maximum or benefit. | “Value” and the commitment have agreed criteria. | Clarify the actual deliverable and limit, without inventing a metric. | [X12] |
| C24 | 幅広い | The claimed range of services has no scope. | The range is enumerated and relevant. | Name the services or fields if supplied. | [X12] |
| C25 | 多くの実績 | “Many” has no count or examples. | Records or cited examples support the volume. | Give the supplied count or examples; otherwise flag it. | [X12] |
| C26 | 誠実な対応 | Self-praise replaces an observable practice. | The document explains how requests or corrections are handled. | Name that practice if documented; do not invent one. | [X12] |
| C27 | 質の高い | A quality claim lacks a criterion or evidence. | Quality criteria and supporting work are stated. | Name the criterion or evidence if supplied; otherwise flag it. | [X12] |
| C28 | 読者目線 | The intended reader and resulting writing choice are unclear. | The audience and editorial decision are explicit. | Name the known audience or decision. | [X12] |
| C29 | ニーズに合わせて | A promise to adapt omits the needs and action. | The needs and adaptation are described. | State the documented response to those needs; otherwise flag it. | [X12] |
| C30 | 最適なソリューション | “Optimal” lacks alternatives and evaluation criteria. | A comparison actually establishes the chosen solution. | Calibrate the claim to available criteria; do not imply an unmade comparison. | [X12] |
| C31 | 結果にコミット | The promised result and conditions are unknown. | A concrete, authorized commitment is documented. | Preserve the promise and flag missing terms; never invent a guarantee. | [X12] |
| C32 | 学び続ける姿勢 | A client-facing pitch substitutes a learning attitude for relevant capability. | The role asks for learning and evidence of it is supplied. | State actual learning or delivered work only if documented. | [X12] |
| C33 | 積極的に対応 | The action, response time or scope is not specified. | The expected action and boundary are clear. | Name the documented action; otherwise flag the promise. | [X12] |
| C34 | さらなる成長 | A pitch centers the applicant's growth rather than the client's task. | Learning is itself the purpose of the application. | Keep the aim if relevant; otherwise state the supported task fit. | [X12] |
| C35 | 迅速に対応 | “Quickly” is a material but undefined service promise. | A response time or process is specified and supportable. | State the actual time/process if supplied; otherwise flag it. | [X12] |

The C labels identify **the reported wording**, not a recommendation to make a document less precise, less formal or more personal. In particular, C01-C03 are ordinary words; [another poster][X10] criticizes one use of 効く because the target of the effect was unclear. In a self-PR, the [article][X12] criticizes generic claims and poor fit to a job; it does not make the fourteen strings globally unsuitable.

Use the following subgroup guidance before considering any edit. A term's appearance alone never triggers one.

| S IDs | Inspect when | Keep when | Safe action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S01-S06 | A legal or literary noun is imported into everyday prose without a clear sense. | Legal precision, a defined term or deliberate literary voice calls for it. | Name or define the intended relation from the source; otherwise leave and flag ambiguity. |
| S07-S14 | A technical or business abstraction omits its object, scale or decision criterion. | The project or field has defined the term and it conveys a real distinction. | Give the supported object, scale or criterion; retain precise terms. |
| S15-S22 | An uncommon or evaluative word changes tone or meaning without serving the reader. | Literal, technical or intentionally evaluative use is accurate. | Clarify the referent or explain a needed term; do not flatten the writer's voice. |
| S23-S35 | A bodily or texture image claims closeness, impact or feeling without a basis. | A physical sensation, actual relationship or deliberate image is relevant. | Remove only expendable decoration; retain and flag an unsupported material claim. |
| S36-S47 | An ordinary verb or evaluation lacks its object, baseline or intended relation. | Literal use, supported comparison or concise established phrasing is clear. | Name the documented target or baseline; leave uncertainty visible. |
| S48-S50 | A recommendation sounds conclusive without options, constraints or criteria. | Those inputs justify the recommendation. | Name the supplied criteria or flag the missing decision basis. |
| S51 | An adverb supplies mood but no actual manner of action. | Quietness is literal or the image is deliberate. | Remove only a dispensable flourish; never delete a documented condition. |
| S52-S63 | Elevated diction makes an ordinary fact sound conclusive or grand. | The discipline, evidence or literary voice supports the strength. | Calibrate to the evidence; avoid an automatic simpler synonym. |
| S64-S68 | A coined label implies an unexplained model or advantage. | It is defined and useful for this reader. | Unpack its supported meaning or flag the missing definition. |
| S69-S75 | A verb suggests causal, evidential or interpretive strength absent from the source. | The relation is established and the register fits. | Match the actual evidence and actor; do not turn a suggestion into proof. |
| S76-S82 | IT language is figurative but the actual process or audience is unclear. | It names real software work or an established audience term. | State the documented process or leave a deliberate metaphor. |
